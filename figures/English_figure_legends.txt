Figure 1. Input construction, paired controls and shared-validation model selection.
(a) Observed set membership for the original CGL partition and selection seed 17. Four target-specific subsets S_j contain 12 columns each; their deduplicated union U contains 42 columns. Six column identities are present in two subsets and are highlighted by aligned tracks and filled cells. Columns are ordered within U, not by physical wavelength spacing. This single example is not a wavelength-stability result. (b) The five arms define three paired questions. A reads S_j and B reads U under the restricted component range 1–q; C reads the same U with up to 15 feasible PLS components. Here q=12 for CGL and q=11 for Corn. Each random expansion D_j^(r) retains S_j and adds randomly sampled columns until its input count equals |U|. Dlow and Dwide use the same draw under the restricted and expanded ranges. The B–Dlow and C–Dwide comparisons match per-model column count and component range, not the global acquisition union, correlation structure or total search cost. (c) The same validation subset V guides both subset search and component choice; candidate PLS1 models are fitted on F, and selected specifications are refitted on F+V. All new selections and refitted models across all new units are frozen before any new test prediction or scoring. Original A–C records are reused; new Corn partitions repeat subset search. Shared V is an internal-selection limitation, not evidence by itself of test leakage. The supplementary controls do not constitute a new independent material-source validation.

Figure 2. Paired macro-average R² differences between union inputs and random expansions.
(a) Every open marker shows Δ_r=macro-R²_union−macro-R²_D,r for one of ten complete random-expansion workflows under the corresponding setting. Squares compare B with Dlow; circles compare C with Dwide. Vertical ticks mark the arithmetic mean of the ten differences. All 80 distinct differences use the same horizontal scale; positive values favour the union. The right-hand columns report mean Δ and the positive/equal/negative counts after the stated aggregation. For the original CGL and Corn settings, seed-specific metrics are first averaged across selection seeds 17, 29 and 43 for each workflow index; the two additional Corn schemes use seed 17. Corn metrics use all 80 concatenated out-of-fold predictions for each seed; CGL uses the 78 official test predictions. Macro-average R² equally weights the four targets. Small vertical offsets separate markers and have no statistical meaning. (b,c) The two CGL comparisons are enlarged with a common, explicitly labelled ×10⁻⁴ scale. These windows repeat the CGL points in panel a, without adding observations. Counts and means describe algorithmic workflows on fixed data; they are not independent-source win probabilities, confidence intervals or significance tests. D retains the target subset and may also sample columns present in U; it is a random-expansion reference, not an uninformative negative control.

Figure 3. Signed partition-sensitivity differences for Corn at selection seed 17.
Rows give B−A, C−B and C−mean(Dwide); columns give the original five-fold partition and the additional five-fold schemes 31001 and 31002. Cell values are differences in macro-average held-out R², printed to five decimal places. The colour scale is centred at zero and spans −0.03 to +0.03 for all cells. Dwide is the arithmetic mean of ten workflow-level metrics from the same partition and seed. Each underlying metric uses all 80 concatenated out-of-fold predictions before target-level macro-averaging. The original-partition cell values use seed 17 alone and therefore differ from the three-seed averages in Figure 2. B−A changes sign, while C−B is positive in these three recorded schemes. All schemes reuse the same 80 samples, and the additional schemes were evaluated after the original results were known. The matrix describes partition sensitivity, not three independent material sources, a temporal trend or a significance test.

Figure 4. Target-specific error costs under the original partitions.
Panels show (a) CGL and (b) Corn, with a common logarithmic horizontal scale. Squares denote RMSE_B/RMSE_A, circles RMSE_C/RMSE_A, and triangles RMSE_C/RMSE_Dwide. The aligned numeric columns give each displayed ratio to three decimal places. RMSE is calculated separately for each selection seed and averaged across the three seeds; Dwide additionally averages the ten random-expansion workflows. Ratios are calculated only after this aggregation, not by averaging replicate-level ratios. A ratio below one favours the numerator; a ratio above one favours the denominator. The reference line at one represents equal RMSE. The increases in Corn moisture error relative to A and the Corn starch cost of C relative to Dwide are retained. Vertical offsets distinguish the three comparisons within each target. These dimensionless ratios describe original-partition results and do not pool the three Corn partition schemes. Absolute RMSE values are supplied in the companion source-data table and the manuscript tables. No significance markers or retraining confidence intervals are added.

Figure S1. CGL validation profiles and the permitted component ranges.
Rows represent casein, glucose, lactate and moisture in the original CGL partition. Left panels show the full available component range; right panels show the 8–15 detail. Curves labelled A use target-specific subsets, whereas B/C curves use the same union inputs. Solid, dashed and dotted lines distinguish selection seeds 17, 29 and 43. Every seed-specific curve is retained; no average curve replaces them. The background band identifies integer component counts 13–15, available only to the expanded union rule; A ends at 12. The vertical scale is common across all four targets within each column, while full and detail columns have different scales. All 24 distinct input curves and 324 saved candidate validation scores are represented; the detail panels repeat their applicable points. Actual A, B and C choices are indexed separately in Figure S3, replacing the multiple selection-marker types used in the earlier curve layout. Values are validation R² on the original F/V partitions, not outer-test scores. Neither a boundary choice nor a flat validation profile establishes convergence or global optimality of 15 components.

Figure S2. Corn validation profiles organised by target with common within-target scales.
Four coordinated sheets show moisture, oil, protein and starch, respectively, for the original five-fold partition. Each sheet has five fold rows and two columns: full range on the left and 8–15 detail on the right. Local labels a–j identify the target-specific fold–window cells within each sheet. Curves labelled A use target-specific subsets, and B/C curves use the same union input. Solid, dashed and dotted lines distinguish selection seeds 17, 29 and 43. All seed-specific curves are drawn individually. Within a target, all five folds share the same vertical limits for a given window; limits are set from all scores in that target and window, with five per cent padding on each side. Full and detail windows use different vertical scales, and scales may differ across targets. The background band identifies counts 12–15, available only to the expanded union rule; A ends at 11. Each sheet retains 30 distinct input curves and 390 full-range candidate scores, giving 120 curves and 1,560 distinct scores overall. Detail views repeat existing points, including the negative starch score, rather than adding experiments. Actual selected counts, including choices below eight, are reported in Figure S3. Curves contain validation R², not test performance or retraining confidence bands; folds and seeds are not independent sources.

Figure S3. Index of actual selected PLS component counts and boundary selections.
(a) CGL choices on the official partition; rows identify four targets. (c–f) Corn choices for moisture, oil, protein and starch, respectively; rows identify the five original outer folds. In each matrix, columns are grouped by selection seeds 17, 29 and 43, with A, B and C in that order within each group. All 216 saved choices are shown as integer counts. Shaded bold cells identify B selecting its permitted maximum q, or C selecting 15; shading denotes these boundary events rather than prediction accuracy or a continuous colour scale. The restricted maximum q is 12 for CGL and 11 for Corn. (b) Descriptive counts summarise B=q, C=15 and C>q over 12 CGL and 60 Corn target–fold–seed records. These denominators comprise dependent target pairs, not independent experiments. This index complements the validation profiles in Figures S1 and S2 without adding fits, seeds, folds or candidate components. It displays realised choices and their variation; it does not establish selection stability, a globally sufficient search range or superior generalisation.
